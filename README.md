# vendor-risk-it-provider-supplier-hacked
ZIA Networks explains fourth-party risk and how to protect your business when your IT provider's supplier is breached.
You vetted your IT provider. You signed the contract, checked their certifications, and made sure they take security seriously.

Then one morning, you learn that a company you've never heard of, one of your provider's own suppliers, has been hacked. Suddenly your data, your systems, or your uptime is at risk.

At **ZIA Networks**, we see this more often than most businesses expect. It's called **fourth-party risk**, and it's one of the most underestimated threats facing organizations today.

## The chain behind your IT provider

Most companies think about their own defenses and those of their direct vendors. The real picture is longer:

| Level | Who they are |
|---|---|
| **You** | The business |
| **Third party** | Your IT provider or managed service provider (MSP) |
| **Fourth party** | The software, cloud platforms, and tools your provider depends on |
| **Fifth party and beyond** | The suppliers behind those tools |

Every link is a possible entry point. Attackers increasingly target the weakest link rather than attacking well-defended companies head-on.

## It has happened before

- **SolarWinds (2020):** Malicious code inserted into an IT management product reached thousands of organizations through routine updates.
- **Kaseya (2021):** A flaw in remote management software used by MSPs let ransomware spread to the MSPs' customers.
- **MOVEit (2023):** A vulnerability in a popular file transfer tool exposed data from organizations that never dealt with the software maker directly.
- **CDK Global (2024):** A ransomware attack on one software provider disrupted thousands of car dealerships at once.

In each case, many victims had no direct relationship with the original point of compromise.

## What it means for you

1. **Data exposure.** Data held by the compromised supplier may be accessed or stolen.
2. **Service disruption.** Monitoring, backup, or support tools may go offline.
3. **Credential theft.** Attackers who reach your provider's tools may obtain credentials that open your network.
4. **Malicious updates.** A compromised supplier can push infected software that your provider unknowingly installs.
5. **Regulatory exposure.** You may still need to notify customers or regulators.
6. **Reputational damage.** Customers rarely care whose fault it was. They remember that *your* business was affected.

## Why it's so hard to see

- You may not know which suppliers your provider uses.
- News of an incident can reach you days or weeks late.
- Contracts often don't say who handles what when a breach starts upstream.
- A yearly security questionnaire is only a snapshot in time.

## How to reduce your exposure

You can't eliminate supply chain risk, but you can manage it.

### 1. Map your dependencies
Ask your provider which tools, platforms, and subcontractors touch your data or systems.

### 2. Strengthen your contracts
Include breach notification timelines (for example, 24 to 72 hours), security requirements that flow down to their suppliers, audit rights, and clear incident response responsibilities.

### 3. Apply least privilege
Limit what your provider and their tools can access. Use separate accounts and require multi-factor authentication everywhere.

### 4. Monitor continuously
Don't rely on annual reviews alone. Watch for security news affecting your vendors.

### 5. Keep independent backups
Store backups your provider's primary tools can't alter or delete, and test restores regularly.

### 6. Plan for the incident
Know who to call, how to cut off vendor access quickly, and how you'll communicate with customers.

### 7. Have an exit strategy
Document how you would switch providers or bring critical functions in-house.

## Questions to ask your IT provider

- [ ] Which suppliers have access to our data or systems?
- [ ] How do you assess and monitor your own vendors?
- [ ] How quickly will you notify us if one is compromised?
- [ ] What is your plan if a critical tool becomes unavailable?
- [ ] Can we review your latest SOC 2 or ISO 27001 report?

## The bottom line

Your security is only as strong as the weakest link in your supply chain, and some of those links sit beyond your view.

> **Vendor risk management isn't about distrust. It's about making sure that when something goes wrong somewhere in the chain, your business is ready.**
